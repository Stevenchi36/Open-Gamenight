# Game ideas

Candidates for future games, not yet filtered. Each entry has a one-line pitch, then what it would reuse, what it would need that doesn't exist yet, and its secrets. When a game is picked, write its `PLAN.md` section (see `games/CLAUDE.md`, "Adding a game") and remove it from here.

## Word and prompt games

- **Just One** (co-op): one player guesses a secret word; the others each secretly write a one-word clue, and duplicate clues cancel before the guesser sees them.
  - Reuses: an Imposter-style word library and categories, `SubmitField`.
  - Secrets: the guesser's word; clues until duplicates are removed.
- **Who's Most Likely To…**: show a prompt, everyone votes for a player, reveal the results.
  - Reuses: `PlayerPicker`, `tallyVotes`, `VoteBreakdown`; needs an AI-generated prompt library. The smallest possible game.
- **Fibbage / Balderdash**: show an obscure fact with a blank; everyone writes a fake answer, then votes for the real one. Score for finding the truth and for each player your fake fooled.
  - Needs: a library of facts, AI-generated by admins ahead of time (gameplay hooks get no `services.ai`).
  - Secrets: the real answer; who wrote which answer until the reveal.
- **Wavelength / Spectrum**: the clue-giver secretly sees a target on a scale ("cold ↔ hot") and gives a clue; the others agree on a slider position and score by how close they get.
  - Needs: a library of scale pairs; a dial/slider component (could go in `@wsfg/core/ui`).
  - Secrets: the target position.
- **Two Truths and a Lie**: each player types three statements about themselves; the others vote for the lie. No content library.
- **Wits & Wagers** (trivia with numeric answers): everyone guesses a number ("How tall is the Eiffel Tower?"), then bets on which guess is closest. Someone who knows nothing can still win by betting well.
  - Needs: an AI-generated question library.
  - Secrets: guesses until everyone has submitted.
- **Wordle Battle** (speed race, 2–12 players): everyone races to solve the same hidden word, Wordle-style. Your score is your solve time plus a time penalty for each guess, so fewer guesses still pays off. Each player sees every opponent's board as colours only (correct / misplaced / not in word) plus their attempt count.
  - Not limited to 5 letters: the word's length varies per round, maybe with min/max length settings for the host. The board takes its width from the word.
  - The word's category is shown as a hint, using the core `categories` table with this game's own categories.
  - Library: a `<gameId>_words` table referencing `categories`, with MCP tools for adding and managing words (like the `imposter_*` tools), and maybe AI generation for admins.
  - Reuses: the categories admin page, `useCountdown`/`serverNow` for timing, `ScoreBoard`.
  - Secrets: the target word; every player's guessed letters (opponents get only the colours). Leak-test both.
  - Open questions: how to validate a guess when lengths vary (a dictionary per length, or accept any letters); duplicate-letter colouring (use Wordle's rule); max guesses (fixed, or scaled with word length); what happens to players who don't solve it (a cap or time limit, then a penalty score).

## Card games with hidden hands

- **6 nimmt!**: everyone secretly picks a card at the same time; cards are revealed and placed onto rows in order, and whoever is forced to take a row collects penalty points. Plays 2–10, with no waiting for turns.
- **Sushi Go**: pick one card from your hand and pass the rest along, over three rounds; simple scoring combos.
- **Love Letter**: a 16-card deck, one card in hand; draw, play one, use its effect to knock others out. Short rounds, 2–6 players.
- **Coup**: two hidden role cards each; claim any role's action, and others may challenge the claim.
- **Hanabi** (co-op): you see everyone's cards except your own, and give limited hints to build fireworks in order. That inverts the usual secrecy, which makes it a good `runLeakTest` case.
- **Cheat / BS**: play cards face down, claim what they are, and anyone can call bluff.
- **No Thanks!**: take the face-up card or pay a chip to pass. Plays up to 7.
- **For Sale**: bid for properties, then sell them off in a second phase.

Card games would justify adding shared deck, shuffle and deal helpers to `@wsfg/core/kit`.

## Dice and push-your-luck

- **Skull**: players secretly stack flower or skull discs, then bid how many flowers they can flip without hitting a skull. Tiny rules and lots of hidden state; a natural follow-up to Liar's Dice that could share its turn and bidding helpers.
- **Farkle / Ten Thousand**: roll, keep scoring dice, and choose between banking points and rolling again at the risk of losing them. No hidden information, so the easiest game to build.
- **Can't Stop**: push-your-luck climbing up columns on a simple board.
- **Yahtzee with friends**: everyone fills in a scorecard from shared or individual rolls.

## Numbers and simultaneous choices

- **Guess 2/3 of the average**: everyone picks 0–100; whoever is closest to two-thirds of the average wins.
- **Lowest unique number**: the lowest number nobody else picked wins.
- **The Mind** (co-op): the group must play their numbered cards in ascending order without talking, purely by timing. Uses `serverNow`, and tests timer and latency handling.
- **Split or Steal**: secret choices made in pairs; quick, and good between rounds of something longer.
- **Rock-Paper-Scissors tournament**: brackets and simultaneous throws, for any group size.

## Social deduction

- **Werewolf / Mafia**: secret roles, night actions and day votes. Fits the secrecy model well, but has many phases and needs a moderator, so the state machine gets large.
- **Secret Hitler-style**: hidden teams, with a vote on every proposal.

## Harder fits

- **Telephone Pictionary (Gartic Phone)**: players alternate between drawing and describing. Needs a drawing canvas, and storing strokes as JSON makes for large state.
- **Pictionary**: same canvas need.
- **Map pin guessing** (GeoGuessr-like): needs a map component.
- **Reflex games** ("tap first"): network lag makes them unfair without careful timing.
